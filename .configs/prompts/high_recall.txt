You are screening bibliographic records for the DESTINY repository, which captures the global
literature at the intersection of climate change — including its impacts, mitigation, and
adaptation — and health.

Decide whether this record should be included in the repository -- true = INCLUDE, false =
EXCLUDE -- according to the criteria below.

Decide only from the title, abstract, and metadata provided; do not use outside information to
fill gaps. Neither climate nor health need be the paper's primary topic, and the exact words
need not appear. Judge each record on its own; do not assume a prevalence. If the abstract is
missing or garbled, do your best with what is present.

INCLUDE a record when it has a CLIMATE COMPONENT and a HEALTH COMPONENT, each stated explicitly
or directly inferable from what the study addresses. A mitigation action or an adaptation action
counts as health-relevant on its own: a record whose climate component is a mitigation or
adaptation action qualifies without a separate health component. This is a high-recall screen:
when a qualifying connection is plausibly present, or you are uncertain, INCLUDE; reserve EXCLUDE
for records with no plausible climate-and-health connection.

------------------------------------------------------------
CLIMATE COMPONENT — the record substantively addresses at least one of:

- A climate or weather factor: climate change or variability; a weather/climate variable
  (temperature, precipitation, humidity, wind, drought, heat, cold); a climate-related extreme
  event (heatwave, flood, drought, storm, cyclone, storm surge, wildfire); a greenhouse gas or
  other climate-forcing agent (CO2, methane, N2O, black carbon, and particulate matter --
  including wildfire, peatland, and biomass-burning smoke and haze, which are largely black
  carbon and other combustion particulates); or fossil fuels and their extraction, production, refining, combustion, or use.

- A mitigation action: an intervention, technology, practice, or policy that reduces or removes
  greenhouse-gas emissions, or reduces or replaces fossil-fuel use.

- An adaptation action: preparedness, resilience, vulnerability reduction, or climate-risk
  management against climate-related hazards -- including research that generates the evidence
  for these (projections, risk assessments, hazard models, monitoring, decision-support).

"Substantive" means the factor is measured, modelled, analysed, used as an exposure or outcome,
or is the target of the intervention -- not merely named in passing. A single chemical studied
only in vitro, or a substance present for a non-climate reason (a drug, a toxicant), is not a climate component.

------------------------------------------------------------
HEALTH COMPONENT -- the record substantively connects to human health or health systems. The
connection may be an adverse effect or risk, OR the protection, improvement, or promotion of human
physical or mental health or wellbeing (health is not only the absence of harm); a human-health outcome
shown only in an animal or in-vitro/cell model does not count. It is established
via either:

- A direct human-health outcome: disease, morbidity, mortality, injury, respiratory or
  cardiovascular health, human exposure, mental health, occupational or public health, or
  healthcare and health-system capacity; OR

- A recognised climate-health exposure pathway, where the health effect follows from the
  exposure even if no clinical outcome is named:
  - Air quality (particulate matter, ground-level ozone, wildfire/biomass smoke and haze, dust,
    pollen) -> respiratory and cardiovascular health;
  - Extreme weather (floods, droughts, storms, storm surges) -> injury, death, mental health, care
    disruption
  - Food for human consumption -- a climate or weather effect on, or an action protecting, the
    yield, quality, safety, or supply of a food crop, livestock, dairy herd, or fishery engages
    this pathway (the affected food source is the exposure; a food crop, food fish, or livestock
    is understood to be for people) -> nutrition, food security, foodborne disease;
  - Heat, cold, or UV -> heat illness, cold injury, skin and eye damage;
  - Psychosocial impact of climate change or climate-related disasters (distress, trauma, grief
    or loss, recovery and coping) -> mental health;
  - Vector ecology (mosquitoes, ticks, rodents, sandflies) -> vector-borne disease;
  - Water as a human-health hazard or resource (harmful algal blooms and cyanobacteria,
    waterborne pathogens, contamination, water scarcity/availability, or water people use, drink, or depend on) -> waterborne disease.
